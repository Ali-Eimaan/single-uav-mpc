\section{Statement}

A control system is \emph{differentially flat} if there exist flat outputs
$\bm\sigma(t)$ such that every state and input is a function of $\bm\sigma$ and finitely
many of its derivatives. For the quadrotor model of \texttt{quadrotor\_se3\_dynamics.tex}
the flat output is the position--yaw triple
\begin{equation}
  \bm\sigma = (x, y, z, \psi) \in \R^3 \times S^1,
  \label{eq:flatout}
\end{equation}
with the thrust $T$ and attitude $R$ recoverable from $(\ddot{\bm p}, \ddot\psi)$ and the
body rates from $(\dddot{\bm p}, \dddot\psi)$. The claim holds provided thrust is strictly
positive, $\|\ddot{\bm p} + g\bm e_3\| > 0$: a free-falling vehicle has an undefined
attitude. This is the structure Mellinger \& Kumar (2011) exploit: a trajectory
$\bm\sigma(t) \in C^4$ maps to a dynamically feasible state--input trajectory without
solving any differential equation.

\section{Recovering attitude}

The vehicle must produce the specific force
\begin{equation}
  \bm t = \ddot{\bm p} + g \bm e_3, \qquad \bm e_3 = (0,0,1),
  \label{eq:tvec}
\end{equation}
pointing along the body thrust axis:
\begin{equation}
  \bm z_B = \frac{\bm t}{\|\bm t\|}.
  \label{eq:zb}
\end{equation}
The heading constraint fixes a body axis relative to a yaw-anchored intermediate frame. With
$\bm x_C = (\cos\psi, \sin\psi, 0)^{\mathsf T}$ the yaw direction in the horizontal plane:
\begin{align}
  \bm y_B &= \frac{\bm z_B \times \bm x_C}{\|\bm z_B \times \bm x_C\|},
  \label{eq:yb} \\
  \bm x_B &= \bm y_B \times \bm z_B,
  \label{eq:xb}
\end{align}
and $R = [\bm x_B \ \bm y_B \ \bm z_B] \in \SO$. \Cref{eq:yb} makes $\bm y_B$ horizontal by
construction and orthogonal to $\bm z_B$, so $\bm x_B$ from \eqref{eq:xb} is orthogonal to
both: $R$ is a proper rotation.

\paragraph{Degenerate case.} When $\bm z_B \times \bm x_C \to \bm 0$ — the required thrust
axis is vertical (hover, or any straight-and-level segment) — \eqref{eq:yb} is undefined.
The implementation (\texttt{trajectory\_generator.cpp}, \texttt{flatToStateInput})
perturbs $\psi$ by $10^{-4}$ rad and recomputes, and also guards $\|a + g e_z\| < 10^{-3}$
by falling back to a level attitude with $T = 0$ (free-fall / degenerate branch). This is a
numeric branch, not an approximation: the perturbed solution converges to the same limit.

\section{Recovering thrust}

The collective thrust is the projection of the required force onto the body axis:
\begin{equation}
  T = m \|\bm t\|,
  \label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
so the per-rotor thrusts follow from the allocation inverse
($\bm u = A^{-1}[T; \bm\tau]$, see \texttt{quadrotor\_se3\_dynamics.tex}
\eqref{eq:alloc}). \emph{Without} the rotor-drag term in the model, \eqref{eq:T} is exact
only for drag-free dynamics. With linear drag $\bm F_d = -D R^{\mathsf T} \bm v$ the correct
expression is
\begin{equation}
  T = m \left\| \ddot{\bm p} + g \bm e_3 + \frac{1}{m} R D R^{\mathsf T} \dot{\bm p}
  \right\|,
  \label{eq:Tdrag}
\end{equation}
a fixed point because $R$ and hence the drag direction depend on $\bm t$ which itself
depends on $T$. The implementation intentionally ignores this correction (Faessler et al.\
2017 discuss it): the model used by the generator is the drag-free flat map. This is a
modelling error that shows up as a systematic lag on fast trajectories — the honest caveat
is stated in \texttt{nmpc\_formulation.tex} \S\ref{sec:limits} and is visible in the error
vs.\ commanded-acceleration plot of \texttt{analysis/tracking\_error\_analysis.ipynb}.

\section{Recovering body rates from jerk}

Differentiate the translational equation of motion once. With $T = m \|\bm t\|$ and
$\bm z_B = \bm t/\|\bm t\|$:
\begin{equation}
  m \dddot{\bm p} = \dot T \bm z_B + T \dot{\bm z}_B
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \dot T = m \bm z_B \cdot \dddot{\bm p},
  \label{eq:Tdot}
\end{equation}
because $\bm z_B \perp \dot{\bm z}_B$. The perpendicular component defines
\begin{equation}
  \bm h = \dot{\bm z}_B = \frac{m}{T} \left( \dddot{\bm p} - (\bm z_B \cdot
  \dddot{\bm p}) \bm z_B \right),
  \label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
the projection of the jerk onto the plane perpendicular to $\bm z_B$, scaled by $m/T$. The
body-rate components follow from the kinematics $\dot{\bm x}_B = \skewm{\bm\omega} \bm x_B$,
i.e.\ $\bm\omega \cdot \bm y_B = \dot{\bm x}_B \cdot \bm y_B$ etc.:
\begin{align}
  \omega_x &= -\bm h \cdot \bm y_B, \label{eq:wx} \\
  \omega_y &= \bm h \cdot \bm x_B, \label{eq:wy}
\end{align}
using $\dot{\bm z}_B = \bm h$. The yaw rate enters through the constraint that $\bm y_B$ and
$\bm x_B$ rotate with the commanded heading:
\begin{equation}
  \omega_z = \frac{\dot\psi (\bm x_C \cdot \bm x_B) + \omega_y (\bm y_C \cdot \bm z_B)}
    {\|\bm y_C \times \bm z_B\|},
  \label{eq:wz}
\end{equation}
with $\bm y_C = (-\sin\psi, \cos\psi, 0)^{\mathsf T}$. These three equations are exactly
the implementation at \texttt{trajectory\_generator.cpp}
(\texttt{flatToStateInput}, lines 300--315): the projection onto $\bm x_B, \bm y_B$ is the
step where implementations go wrong — using the raw jerk instead of its perpendicular
projection $\bm h$ misattributes the thrust-rate component to a body rate.

\section{Recovering angular acceleration from snap}

Differentiate \eqref{eq:h} once more to get $\ddot{\bm z}_B = \bm h'$, then propagate the
product rule through \eqref{eq:yb}--\eqref{eq:xb} to obtain $R'$ and $R''$. The
angular-acceleration matrix follows from differentiating $[\bm\omega]_\times = R^{\mathsf T}
R'$:
\begin{equation}
  [\dot{\bm\omega}]_\times = R'^{\mathsf T} R' + R^{\mathsf T} R'',
  \label{eq:wdot}
\end{equation}
and the required body torque from Euler's equation:
\begin{equation}
  \bm\tau = J \dot{\bm\omega} + \bm\omega \times J \bm\omega,
  \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
finally $\bm u = A^{-1} [T; \bm\tau]$. The implementation follows
\eqref{eq:wdot} literally: \texttt{omega\_dot\_hat = R\_prime.transpose() * R\_prime +
R.transpose() * R\_pp} (\texttt{trajectory\_generator.cpp} lines 370--372). Note the
implementation also carries $\dot T$ and the yaw-acceleration $\ddot\psi$ through the same
product-rule chain; \Cref{eq:wdot} is the condensed form.

\section{Feasibility}
\label{sec:feas}

A flat trajectory is dynamically feasible iff the recovered input lies in the actuator set
for all $t$. The implementation's \texttt{isDynamicallyFeasible()} samples at 200 Hz over
one period (plus ramp-in) and checks
\begin{equation}
  \max_t \| \dot{\bm p}(t) \| \le v_{\max} (1 + 10^{-3}),
  \qquad
  \max_t \| \ddot{\bm p}(t) \| \le a_{\max} (1 + 10^{-3}),
  \label{eq:feas}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  u_{\min}(1 - 10^{-3}) \le \max_t \frac{m \|\ddot{\bm p}(t) + g \bm e_3\|}{4}
  \le u_{\max}(1 + 10^{-3}),
  \label{eq:feas2}
\end{equation}
using the collective-only bound $\max_t m\|\bm t\|/4$ (the torque trim is neglected; a
conservative check on a vehicle with ample torque margin).

\paragraph{Worked example: the 3 m / 5 s figure-8 on the X500.} With
$x = A_x \sin \omega t$, $y = \frac{A_y}{2}\sin 2\omega t$, $\omega = 2\pi/5 \approx
1.257$ rad/s, $A_x = A_y = 3$ m, $m = 2.0$ kg, $g = 9.80665$ m/s$^2$:
\begin{align*}
  \max_t \|\dot{\bm p}\| &= A_x \omega \sqrt{2} = 3 \cdot 1.257 \cdot 1.414
    \approx 5.33\ \text{m/s} \le 8.0\ \text{m/s} \ \text{(OK)}, \\
  \max_t \|\ddot{\bm p}\| &\approx 10.07\ \text{m/s}^2 \le 18.0\ \text{m/s}^2
    \ \text{(OK)}, \\
  \max_t \frac{m \|\ddot{\bm p} + g \bm e_3\|}{4} &= \frac{2.0 \cdot \sqrt{10.07^2 +
    9.80665^2}}{4} \approx 7.03\ \text{N} \le 8.55\ \text{N} \ \text{(OK)},
\end{align*}
with peak bank $\arctan(\max\|\ddot{\bm p}\|/g) \approx 45.8^\circ$. The thrust-to-weight
ratio of the X500 is $4 \times 8.55 / (2.0 \times 9.80665) = 1.74$; the figure-8 uses
$7.03/8.55 = 82\%$ of the available per-rotor thrust at its peak, which is why the
aggressive preset is the stress case for the README GIF but not a clipped one.

\section{Minimum-snap trajectory generation}

The flatness map consumes $\bm\sigma$ up to the fourth derivative (the snap enters through
\eqref{eq:wdot} via $R''$), so it is natural to generate trajectories that minimise snap
while enforcing position and derivative continuity. For a single axis $p(t)$
piecewise-polynomial with $M$ segments of order 7 (a minimum-jerk solution has order 5;
minimum-snap needs order 7 to make the fourth derivative free):
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\{p_j(t)\}} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \int_0^{T_j} \left( p_j^{(4)}(t) \right)^2 dt,
  \qquad
  \text{s.t. } p_j^{(k)}(T_j) = p_{j+1}^{(k)}(0),\ k = 0..4,
  \label{eq:snapQP}
\end{equation}
with the boundary conditions $p_1^{(k)}(0)$ and $p_M^{(k)}(T_M)$ fixed by the start and goal
states. This is a convex QP in the polynomial coefficients; Mellinger \& Kumar (2011) solve
it directly and Richter, Bry \& Roy (2013) give the unconstrained reformulation used for
waypoint-time allocation. The $C^4$ segment-to-segment continuity in
\eqref{eq:snapQP} is exactly what \texttt{buildMinimumSnap()} enforces in
\texttt{trajectory\_generator.cpp}, and it is the same continuity the flatness map needs:
a discontinuity in $\bm\sigma^{(4)}$ would appear as a jump in the commanded torque.
